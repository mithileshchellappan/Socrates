# Panes

The Mac main window's side panes (docs/DESIGN.md 6.4) and the floating panel the agent's small chat lives in. Platform UI: AppKit is allowed here and nowhere else in the milestone. The app is the only client. Feature screens never import this package: they use StudyUI's pane vocabulary (`Packages/StudyUI/Sources/StudyUI/Panes.swift`). Every source is `#if os(macOS)`; an iOS build sees an empty module.

## Shape

- **`PaneHost`** (SwiftUI) wraps the screen's navigation stack in the detail column of the window's `NavigationSplitView`. It is a `Layout` of [screen | strip] (`PaneHostLayout`): the strip is clamp(the columns' widths + dividers, `placedNeed`, width − the screen's minimum), the columns' minimums winning over the screen's (an `NSSplitView` cannot go under them); the screen gets the rest. Both start under the toolbar; the screen keeps its own reach under it. The screen gets `\.isLiveResizing`, `\.mainIsCovered` and `\.paneControl`, and its `.pane(…)` offers are read here.
- **The strip** (`PaneStrip`, `PaneStripController`) is an `NSSplitViewController` on a `PaneSplitView` (hairline dividers on the panel surface, hit 3 pt either side), one item per column; every column is a vertical `PaneSplitController` of its stacked panes (each at least 36 + 120 pt, heights kept as fractions). A 5 pt `PaneEdgeHandle` at its leading edge drags the first column; it stops where the screen reaches its minimum.
  - Widths: each column's maximum is the student's width and its holding priority falls with its width, so when the strip narrows the widest column gives way first (to its minimum, then the next widest), and when it widens again each column grows back to the student's width. During a divider drag the two columns beside it take the change; during the edge drag, the first column. Only AppKit views lay out during a divider drag.
  - A column whose panes did not change keeps its views in place when others open, close or move (they never leave the window).
- **`PaneItemController`**, one per open pane, pooled for the window by `PaneStage` (so a pane moves between columns, screen kinds and the maximize slot without being built again; closing empties its root and drops it). It holds a `PaneView`: the AppKit `PaneHeaderView` (36 pt, title 12.5 pt semibold or the offer's accessory in a small `NSHostingView`, ⤢ / ⤡ and ✕ as 24 × 24 `PaneHeaderButton`s with a radius 6 hover fill, tooltips "Maximize ⇧⌘↩", "Restore ⇧⌘↩", "Close") over an `NSHostingController` (`sizingOptions = []`, `sceneBridgingOptions = []`, `safeAreaRegions = []`). Its `PaneItemView` stays in the column and stands in for the pane (the bare surface) while it is maximized.
- **`PaneRoot`** is each pane's SwiftUI root: the host's whole environment (captured on every strip update into `PaneEnvironment`) as `.environment(\.self, …)`, then `\.isLiveResizing`, `\.paneIsMaximized` and `\.paneControl`. The offer's content is installed again only when the offer's identity changes. A pane open before its offer arrives shows its header and surface.
- **`PaneStage`** is the window's AppKit side, which `PaneHost` owns: the pool, the strip, the maximize slot and `PaneWindowWatcher` (the window's width, live resize and first responder, for the model). On every strip update it installs offers, updates headers, arranges the columns, moves a maximized pane into the slot (`PaneSlotView`, a 0.2 s `CATransition` fade; the pane's view moves without leaving the window) or back, takes the keyboard from a pane that closes, folds or is covered, and runs a due `focus`.
- **`PaneHostModel`** holds one window's panes: offered specs, open panes, the maximized pane, the needs (`placedNeed`, `requestedNeed`, `requestedMinimum`), the live-resize flag, the pane whose text has the keyboard (`typingPane`). Requests: `open(_:focus:)`, `close`, `toggle`, `toggleMaximized`; `control` (one value for the model's lifetime) exposes them to screens as `\.paneControl`. The room rules are below. `PaneArrangement` is the columns laid out now, derived from the layout by those rules.
- **`PaneSpec`** describes a pane (title, menu title, glyph, widths, opens by default, open per window, title or accessory in the header). The app keeps the table of which screen kind offers which panes.
- **`PaneLayout`** is Codable and kept per screen kind under `Socrates.panes.<kind>`: the columns (stacks of panes, open or closed), their widths and stack fractions, the open panes and the maximized one. Window-wide open panes are stored under `Socrates.panes.windowOpen`. `PaneLayoutStore` reads and writes both.
- **`PaneToolbarToggles`** is one glass group, one toolbar `Toggle` per offered pane (the agent last; the window draws a lit toggle accent soft with an accent glyph; `.imageScale(.small)`; help "Notes ⌥⌘1"). A folded pane's toggle shows unlit. A toggle restores a maximized layout first; a closed pane's toggle then opens it too. It is the last of the app's trailing items.
- **`PaneCommands`** gives View ▸ Show / Hide each pane (⌥⌘1…⌥⌘9 in toolbar order) and Maximize Pane / Restore Pane (⇧⌘↩; Restore is off while the maximized pane has no column to go back to). Commands find the window's model through `.paneCommandsTarget(_:)`.
- **`.floatingPanel(isPresented:frame:content:)`** places `FloatingPanelView`, an AppKit view of the panel's own size plus a 6 pt resize band, at the stored frame (offset from the content area's bottom-trailing corner, 12 pt margins). Moving: `\.floatingPanelMover`'s `changed` sets only the view's layer translation (clamped, actions off); `ended` writes the frame once and the translation is cleared in the update where SwiftUI places the view there. Resizing: the 4 edges and 4 corners (12 pt squares) with `NSCursor.frameResize(position:directions:)`, live (the content sees `\.isLiveResizing`), 280 × 160 at least, the content area less its margins at most, the frame written once at the end. Look: the panel surface clipped at radius 14, a 0.5 pt ring (black 12 % / white 14 %), the panel shadow (0 18 40, spread −14 / −12) on its own layer with a `shadowPath`. It fades in and out in 0.15 s, is kept inside the window when the window changes, and takes no event outside its rectangle and band (no layer covers the window).

## Room rules (nothing closes another pane; the window never grows)

1. Opening: when the open columns at the student's widths fit beside the sidebar and the screen's minimum, the pane opens at its width.
2. Else, when the window holds the columns' minimums without the sidebar: `requestedNeed` / `requestedMinimum` ask the app's sidebar fit to hide the sidebar, and the column is placed in the update the room shows the sidebar gone (a safety limit of 400 ms places it as the room allows should the sidebar stay). The widest pane gives way toward its minimum.
3. Else the pane opens maximized only, without a column; ⤡ is off until the window has room, and the column then appears.
- A width the window imposes (a tile, full screen, a screen with a larger minimum, the student's own sidebar) folds the most recently opened columns. They stay open and come back when the room returns; their toggles show unlit, and `\.paneControl.isOpen` is false for them. Clicking a folded toggle applies the rules again.
- `placedNeed` is the minimums and dividers of the columns laid out now (the app adds it to the detail column's and the split view's minimums). `requestedNeed` is the student's widths of the columns that fit without the sidebar; `requestedMinimum` their minimums. The sidebar fit hides the sidebar while W − screen − sidebar < `requestedNeed` and W − screen ≥ `requestedMinimum`, and brings it back once W − screen − sidebar − 40 ≥ `requestedNeed` or the need is 0.

## Rules

1. Pane content reads the screen's state only through `@Observable` objects its offer captures. Values, bindings, `@FocusState`, the screen's environment and preferences do not cross hosting views.
2. A pane's content closure is called again only when its offer's identity changes.
3. Layouts are written only when a student's action ends: a toggle, ✕, maximize or restore, a divider, edge, stack or header drag. A width or a maximize the room imposes is never written.
4. Columns never move by SwiftUI animation. Opening changes the layout once, then the pane's layer slides 16 pt in from trailing while fading in (0.18 s, ease out). Closing fades the pane in its column (0.12 s; the model keeps it as `closing` meanwhile, its toggle already unlit), then the layout changes once. Maximize and restore cross-fade the slot in 0.2 s; the floating panel fades in 0.15 s. With Reduce Motion none of these animate.
5. Focus: opening by toggle never moves focus; `open(_:focus: true)` and maximize run the offer's `focus` once its content is in the window (a maximized pane: once it is in the slot). Closing or folding a pane that holds the caret, and maximizing over a view that holds it, gives it up (`makeFirstResponder(nil)`).
6. No tests (the project's rule). Verification is `swift build --package-path Packages/Panes -j 4`, the app build, and renders.

## For the integrator

- The public API is the contracts' (`391e654`) with no signature change. `PaneHostModel.control` is now stored (one value for the model's lifetime, so setting it in the environment never invalidates a view); `dividerThickness` is `nonisolated`.
- The floating panel's content hugs: while `\.floatingPanelFrame?.height` is nil, the content is offered half the window's height and must take only the height it needs (as `AgentPanelView`'s compact thread does with `UpToHeight`); a content that fills any height makes the panel half the window. Its title row's drag goes to `\.floatingPanelMover` in window points (right and down positive); call `ended` however the drag ends. Apply `.floatingPanel` at the window's root, so its corner is the content area's.
- The floating panel's own resize sets `\.isLiveResizing` for its content only (the modifier has no model); the window's live resize and every pane drag set the model's flag.
- Renders: headers, buttons, dividers, the strip's edge, the placeholder and the floating panel's surface draw in `draw(_:)`, so `cacheDisplay` shows them; pane content and the header accessory are hosting views the composite draws. The floating panel's ring, corner clip and shadow are layer properties.
- The panel surface (#f8f8fa / #222225) now has three copies (CaptureUI's inspector background, ChatUI's `PanelSurface`, `PaneColors.surface`); StudyUI should own one.
